How to Read Bitcoin by Session: Asia, Europe and the U.S.

A practical guide to session returns, ranges, unequal durations and the limits of fixed time-zone labels.

Tape Lounge · 2026-10-22

01

For comparison, this site uses fixed KST windows: Asia 08:00–16:00, Europe 16:00–22:00 and the U.S. 22:00–08:00 the next day. These are analytical buckets, not official exchange opens. Crypto trades around the clock, so the labels do not identify who traded or what caused a move.

02

Session return is (last observed close ÷ first candle open − 1) × 100. The high-to-low range describes how far price traveled, not its direction. A session that swings widely but ends near its starting point should not be called a strong uptrend.

03

The windows are unequal: Asia is 8 hours, Europe 6 and the U.S. 10. Comparing cumulative returns alone gives longer windows more time to move. Compare equal-length slices where possible, or use change per hour as a rough pace measure; that adjustment is not risk-adjusted performance.

04

Check where the close sits between the session high and low. A close near the high means more of the move held into the end; a close near the low may mean a rebound faded. This is a descriptive clue, not a forecast.

05

Look at the next one or two hourly candles and the following window to see whether the move continued or quickly reversed. Compare volume and the timing of official releases such as CPI or rate decisions. Timing overlap does not prove that a session caused the move.

06

A live session has less observation time than a completed one. Read its timestamp and status, and do not compare a partial window with a full session as if they were equivalent. The latest regional windows may also fall on different calendar dates.

07

For a simple hypothetical example, +0.8% over 8 hours and +0.8% over 10 hours have the same cumulative return but different pace. The figures are illustrative only. Return, range, closing location and follow-through together provide more context than naming a single session the day’s winner.

08

Session returns are descriptive statistics that sort observed prices by fixed KST windows. They do not reveal who traded, why price moved or what the next session will do. The KST buckets do not shift with U.S. daylight saving time, so they are not the U.S. cash-market schedule. Cross-check exchange data, volume and official releases.
